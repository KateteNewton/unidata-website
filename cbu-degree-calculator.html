Your average score per course decides the class.

Landing exactly on a boundary puts you in the lower class. With 10 courses, 35 points is a Merit and 35.5 is a Distinction, as in the official table below.

Worked example: 12 courses with 3 × A, 5 × B+ and 4 × B gives 12 + 15 + 8 = 35 points. That's an average of 2.92 per course, which falls in the 30.5 – 42 band, so it's a Merit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which years count towards my CBU degree classification? expand_more

For a BSc, the grades from your 3rd and 4th year. If your programme counts more years, such as 3rd to 5th year, add the courses from all of those years. The calculator works the same way however many years you include.

How many points is each grade worth at CBU? expand_more

For a full course: A+ = 5, A = 4, B+ = 3, B = 2, C+ = 1 and C = 0. A half course earns half of that: A+ = 2.5, A = 2.0, B+ = 1.5, B = 1.0, C+ = 0.5 and C = 0.

What average do I need for a Distinction, Merit or Credit? expand_more

Divide your total points by your number of courses. An average above 3.5 is a Distinction, above 2.5 is a Merit, above 1.5 is a Credit, and anything else is a Pass. With 12 courses, for example, you need 42.5 points or more for a Distinction.

How are half courses counted? expand_more

A half course earns half the points of a full course. This calculator also counts it as half a course when working out your average, so two half courses weigh the same as one full course.

Does this calculator work for UNZA or other universities? expand_more

No. It follows the Copperbelt University (CBU) regulations only. Other universities use different grading and classification rules.

Is the result official? expand_more

No. It's an unofficial estimate based on CBU's published rules. Your final degree class is confirmed by the University, so check with your School if anything looks unclear.
